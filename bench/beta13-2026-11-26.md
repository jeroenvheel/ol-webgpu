# Memory and speed of 0.1.0-beta.13 (2026-11-26)

`npm run bench -- --layers=webgl2,webgpu` (three runs), `npm run test:memory`
and `npm run test:memory -- --big` on `main` at `8701028` (0.1.0-beta.13).
AMD Radeon R9 200 Series, D3D11, Playwright Chromium, the machine and the
day of [history-2026-11-26.md](history-2026-11-26.md): the numbers are put
next to those there. One after the other, with 9.7 GB of 16 GB free before
each.

beta.13 changed how labels are laid out, how symbols that take part in
decluttering are drawn, and the order of a `renderOrder`. None of that is in
this benchmark (shapes without labels, no order): it is here to show that
nothing else moved. What beta.13 gained is in its release notes and in
[docs/benchmark.md](../docs/benchmark.md): the worst frame with 200 000
labelled places went from 118 to 15 ms.

## Memory

Each scenario with `ol/Feature` and with RenderFeatures, on both backends:
12 cycles after 3 to warm up.

- 0 checks failed. After disposing everything 0 features, RenderFeatures,
  maps, layers, renderers, canvases and buffers are left in all four passes;
  the heap is 11.3–11.4 MB (WebGL2) and 12.1 MB (WebGPU).
- Heap growth per cycle is −1 to 9 KB, except a whole map made and disposed
  (46–50 KB, which is 0.6 MB once: see
  [loose-ends-2026-11-26.md](loose-ends-2026-11-26.md)).
- The scenario that is new in beta.13, a map whose element is taken from the
  page (`detachMap`), grows by 1 KB per cycle; labels with new texts by 0–4
  KB.

### A big layer

300k points, 200k lines, 50k polygons. Heap in MB:

| | WebGL2 | WebGPU |
|---|---|---|
| the features themselves | 610 | 610 |
| added by the layer | 398 (397 in beta.12) | 233 (233) |
| the same with RenderFeatures | 176 + 280 (280) | 176 + 116 (116) |
| a rebuild | 0.6 s | 0.6 s |

The heap does not grow over four rebuilds (0.2 MB in all) and is back at 8.7
(WebGL2) and 9.1 MB (WebGPU) after everything is dropped.

The GPU process, `ol/Feature`, MB (drawn → rebuilds 1–4): WebGL2 518 → 518 /
518 / 518 / 518; WebGPU 468 → 595 / 590 / 582 / 460. On WebGPU it is one
upload more for a while, as before (see the loose ends).
