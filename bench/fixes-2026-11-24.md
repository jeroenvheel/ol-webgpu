# Fixes after 0.1.0-beta.7, one by one (2026-11-24)

The open points of the comparison with the `webgpu-renderer` branch of
OpenLayers, done one at a time on `fix/picking-labels-order`. After each one:

- `npx tsc --noEmit`, `npx vitest run`, `cargo test` (core),
  `npm run test:rendering` (WebGL2 and WebGPU), `npm run test:memory`;
- `npm run bench -- --layers=webgl2,webgpu` (1M points, 200k lines, 100k
  polygons), one run.

AMD Radeon R9 200 Series, D3D11, Playwright Chromium, on one machine. A
single benchmark run differs by up to about 14% from the next one (see
[beta6-2026-11-23.md](beta6-2026-11-23.md)), so only larger differences mean
something. Times are in ms.

## 1. Picking where symbols and strokes are drawn

**Was.** A symbol was hit within its radius around its point, and a stroke
within half its width of its line, whatever `circle-displacement`,
`shape-displacement`, `icon-displacement`, `icon-anchor`, the rotation of the
symbol or `stroke-offset` said. Nothing was found further than 128 px from a
geometry.

**Confirmed first.** Ten new checks in `test/rendering/picking.ts`, with the
expected answers that ol's own `WebGLVector` layer gives: 9 of the 10 failed
on both backends (a displaced circle and a stroke with an offset were hit at
their geometry and not where they are drawn, an icon anchored at its bottom
was hit below its point, a circle of 150 px was not hit 140 px from its
point).

**Now.** A hit of the index says where the cursor is: relative to the point,
or on which side of the line (6 words per hit instead of 4). The picker
places the symbol as ol's shaders do (displacement, anchor with its origin
and units, rotation, `rotate-with-view`) and tests an icon as a rectangle,
a circle or shape as a circle. A stroke with an offset is hit beside its
line. The search radius is how far symbols and strokes reach, without a
maximum; only while sizes that depend on feature properties are not measured
yet, 128 px is searched.

**Tests.** 25 of 25 picks on both backends (was 15 of 15, and 6 of the 25).
Unit 50 of 50, Rust 14 of 14 (one new), rendering 19 cases with 0 failures
in every group, memory 0 failed.

**Measured.** No difference outside the noise: a pick takes 0.02–0.10 ms as
before, the index is built as fast and is as large (the hits grew, not the
index).

**Not exact.** At a sharp bend of a line with an offset, the hit area
follows the nearest segment and not the drawn join.

## 2. A feature is reported once on a map that shows the world more than once

**Was.** For one pixel ol's map renderer asks a layer three times: for the
coordinate in the world of the projection and one world to the west and to
the east of it. The picker looked in every world that was drawn for each of
those calls, and so did the labels.

**Confirmed first.** Six new checks on a view where the world is 100 px
wide: `forEachFeatureAtPixel` reported the point three times, at its place
in the middle world and in the next one, and the same for its label. (ol's
own `WebGLVector` layer also reports it three times.)

**Now.** A call answers for its coordinate alone. Labels are placed per world
on screen: they are looked for in the call for the world of the projection,
in every world that is drawn. `layer.getFeatures(pixel)` asks the three
places itself and reports a feature once.

**Tests.** 31 of 31 picks on both backends (25 before, 4 of the 6 new ones
failed). Unit 50 of 50, Rust 14 of 14, rendering 19 cases with 0 failures in
every group, memory 0 failed.

**Measured.** No difference outside the noise. (One pick of the WebGL2 point
layer took 0.16 ms in this run against 0.10 ms; the WebGPU layer, with the
same picker, 0.09 ms.)

**Not exact.** A label that is hidden by decluttering in the world that was
clicked, but shown at the same place in another world on screen, is hit.
