# Memory and speed of 0.1.0-beta.12 (2026-11-26)

`npm run test:memory`, `npm run bench -- --layers=webgl2,webgpu` and
`npm run test:memory -- --big` on `main` at `ad0acb9` (0.1.0-beta.12),
Playwright Chromium, D3D11, on mains power. On another machine than the
reports before: a laptop with an Intel Iris Xe and an NVIDIA T550 Laptop GPU,
31 GB of memory (19 GB free before the runs). The times are therefore not to
be put next to those of beta.10 and beta.11 (AMD Radeon R9 200, 16 GB); the
heap is.

On both GPUs one after the other: the memory test, the benchmark twice, the
big layer. On the Intel GPU the benchmark ran twice more at the end, because
its first two runs differed a lot.

## Choosing the GPU

Chromium ignores `powerPreference` on Windows (crbug.com/369219127) and takes
the GPU Windows gives the program. Headless Playwright runs
`chrome-headless-shell.exe`, not `chrome.exe`. For the NVIDIA runs that
program was given the high-performance GPU, and the setting was removed
afterwards:

    HKCU\Software\Microsoft\DirectX\UserGpuPreferences
      <...>\ms-playwright\chromium_headless_shell-1255\chrome-headless-shell-win64\chrome-headless-shell.exe = "GpuPreference=2;"

The `GPU:` line of the benchmark and the WebGPU adapter (`nvidia turing`,
`intel gen-12lp`) were checked before each set of runs.

## Memory

12 cycles after 3 to warm up, each scenario with `ol/Feature` and with
RenderFeatures, on both backends, on both GPUs.

- 0 checks failed. After disposing everything 0 features, RenderFeatures,
  maps, layers, renderers, canvases and buffers are left in all eight
  passes; the heap is 11.2 MB (WebGL2) and 12.0 MB (WebGPU) against 7.8 MB
  for the empty page.
- Heap growth per cycle is −2 to 8 KB, except a whole map made and disposed
  (44–50 KB, 44–49 in beta.11, which is not growth: see
  [loose-ends-2026-11-26.md](loose-ends-2026-11-26.md)).
- Labels with new texts: −1 to 5 KB per cycle (35–42 KB before
  `withoutText`). The fix of beta.12 holds.
- The heap is the same on both GPUs to 0.1 MB in every scenario.

### A big layer

300k points, 200k lines, 50k polygons. Heap added by the layer, the same on
both GPUs:

| | WebGL2 | WebGPU |
|---|---|---|
| `ol/Feature` | 397 MB (398 in beta.11) | 233 MB (233) |
| RenderFeatures | 280 MB (280) | 116 MB (116) |

The heap does not grow over four rebuilds (0.1–0.2 MB in all) and is back at
8.7 (WebGL2) and 9.1 MB (WebGPU) after everything is dropped. A rebuild
takes 0.5–0.8 s.

The GPU process, `ol/Feature`, MB (empty page → drawn → rebuilds 1–4):

| | WebGL2 | WebGPU |
|---|---|---|
| Intel | 81 → 564 → 563 / 563 / 563 / 563 | 230 → 439 → 435 / 435 / 435 / 435 |
| NVIDIA | 73 → 546 → 546 / 546 / 546 / 546 | 388 → 798 → 636 / 774 / 629 / 767 |

On the NVIDIA GPU, WebGPU goes up and down by about 140 MB between rebuilds
and does not grow; that fits the copy `queue.writeBuffer` makes on its way to
the buffer (see the loose ends). On the Intel GPU it does not show.

- **Run 2 on the Intel GPU is the odd one.** Its main-thread steps were up
  to 39% longer than in run 1 (the batch of the lines 371 against 267 ms) and
  its WebGPU ready times 41–83% longer. Runs 3 and 4 are between the two.
  The machine, not the code: the worker stages and the frames of run 2 are
  as in the others.
- **Points on WebGL2 on the Intel GPU: 86–88 ms a frame**, against 24–25 on
  WebGPU, in all four runs. On the NVIDIA GPU both are 20–21 ms, and on the
  AMD GPU of beta.10 both were about 12. Not looked into.
- **Lines on the Intel GPU are slower on WebGPU** (36–38 ms against 31–32).
  On the NVIDIA GPU WebGPU is faster for lines (16 against 20) and polygons
  (11 against 16–19).
- **Ready.** On the NVIDIA GPU, WebGPU draws its first frame before WebGL2
  in all six (14–31% sooner). On the Intel GPU in 7 of 12; the medians are
  500 against 540 ms for points, 498 against 451 for lines and 389 against
  495 for polygons (WebGPU against WebGL2).
- The worker stages are the same on both GPUs, as they should be, and
  faster than on the machine of beta.10 (lines 188–218 against 269 ms, the
  index of points 130–167 against 168–181).
